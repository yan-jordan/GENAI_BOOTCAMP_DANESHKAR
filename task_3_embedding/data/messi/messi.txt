The Story of Lionel Messi: A Chronicle of a Football Career

1. Early Life and Discovery

Lionel Andrés Messi was born on June 24, 1987, in Rosario, Argentina, the third of four children of Jorge Messi, a factory steel worker, and Celia Cuccittini. He began playing football almost as soon as he could walk, joining his first club, Grandoli, coached by his father, before moving at age six to Newell's Old Boys, one of Rosario's biggest clubs, in 1994. At Newell's, Messi played alongside boyhood friends in a youth side so dominant it became known as "The Machine of '87," a reference to the shared birth year of most of its players.

Around age eleven, Messi was diagnosed with a growth hormone deficiency, a condition that required expensive injections his family could not fully afford. Newell's Old Boys initially agreed to help, but funding became uncertain, and the family began looking elsewhere. In September 2000, thirteen-year-old Messi traveled to Barcelona for a trial with FC Barcelona, arranged through a family connection to Argentine agent Josep Maria Minguella. After watching Messi play, Barcelona's sporting director Carles Rexach was convinced enough that, on December 14, 2000, lacking official paper at hand, he wrote the terms of a contract offer on a paper napkin to secure the signing before the family returned to Argentina. That napkin later became one of the most famous documents in football history.

2. La Masia and the Move to Spain

The Messi family relocated to Barcelona in February 2001, settling in an apartment near Camp Nou so that Jorge could be close to his son's development. Lionel enrolled at Barcelona's famed youth academy, La Masia, where he progressed rapidly through the club's junior sides: Infantil, Cadete, and Juvenil. His coaches during this period included youth-team mentors who noted his extraordinary close control and low center of gravity, both partly a product of his small stature. Barcelona also continued to fund his hormone treatment, which Messi later credited as a turning point that allowed him to grow to his adult height of 1.70 meters while completing his physical development.

By 2003, still only sixteen, Messi was training with Barcelona's B team and had begun attracting attention from the reserve and first-team coaching staff, including then-manager Frank Rijkaard. On November 16, 2003, he played his first friendly for the senior side against FC Porto, then managed by José Mourinho.

3. Breaking Into the First Team

Messi made his official first-team debut for FC Barcelona on October 16, 2004, coming on as a substitute against RCD Espanyol in a La Liga match, at seventeen years and four months old, then the youngest player to appear for the club in a competitive match. His first senior goal came on May 1, 2005, against Albacete, when Ronaldinho, Barcelona's Brazilian superstar, flicked a delicate backheel pass into Messi's path for a chip over the goalkeeper, a goal often replayed as the symbolic passing of the torch from one generation's best player to the next.

That same year, Messi captained Argentina's under-20 team to victory at the 2005 FIFA World Youth Championship in the Netherlands, finishing as both the tournament's top scorer and its best player. He made his senior international debut for Argentina on August 17, 2005, in a friendly against Hungary, though he was sent off within minutes of coming on as a substitute. Messi's first World Cup came in Germany in 2006 under coach José Pékerman, though he featured only sparingly as Argentina lost to the hosts' eventual semifinalist Germany in a penalty shootout in the quarterfinals. Barcelona won the 2005-06 Champions League, defeating Arsenal 2-1 in the final in Paris on May 17, 2006, though Messi missed the match itself due to a torn thigh muscle suffered weeks earlier.

4. The Guardiola Era and the First Treble

Pep Guardiola took charge of Barcelona's first team in the summer of 2008, promoting several La Masia graduates and building a side around Messi, Xavi Hernández, and Andrés Iniesta. That same year, Messi helped Argentina win Olympic gold at the Beijing Games, scoring in the final as Argentina beat Nigeria 1-0 on August 23, 2008.

The 2008-09 season became one of the most celebrated in club history. Barcelona won an unprecedented treble of La Liga, the Copa del Rey, and the Champions League. In the Champions League final at Rome's Stadio Olimpico on May 27, 2009, Barcelona defeated Manchester United 2-0, with Messi scoring the second goal with a header, unusual for a player of his height, set up by a cross from Xavi. That December, Messi won his first Ballon d'Or, edging out Cristiano Ronaldo, who had by then left Manchester United for Real Madrid and become Messi's defining career rival.

Guardiola's Barcelona, featuring Carles Puyol, Gerard Piqué, Sergio Busquets, Dani Alves, and Thierry Henry alongside the core trio, went on to dominate Spanish and European football for several more seasons. Barcelona won the Champions League again in 2011, beating Manchester United 3-1 at Wembley Stadium on May 28, 2011, with Messi scoring the second goal. Messi won the Ballon d'Or four years in a row, from 2009 through 2012, a record that stood for years afterward. In 2012, he broke the record for most goals scored in a calendar year, finishing with 91 goals for club and country, surpassing the 85-goal mark set by West Germany's Gerd Müller in 1972.

5. Heartbreak and Redemption with Argentina

Despite his club success, major trophies with Argentina proved elusive for years. At the 2010 World Cup in South Africa, Argentina, coached by Diego Maradona, was eliminated 4-0 by Germany in the quarterfinals. At the 2014 World Cup in Brazil, under coach Alejandro Sabella, Argentina reached the final for the first time since 1990, but lost 1-0 to Germany after Mario Götze's extra-time goal in the final at the Maracanã Stadium in Rio de Janeiro on July 13, 2014. Messi was named winner of the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player, a choice that remained controversial given Argentina's defeat.

More heartbreak followed in the Copa América. Argentina lost the 2015 final to Chile on penalties, and lost the 2016 Copa América Centenario final, also to Chile, again on penalties, on June 26, 2016. After that second consecutive final defeat, an emotionally exhausted Messi announced his retirement from the national team, though he reversed the decision within weeks under pressure from fans and teammates such as Javier Mascherano and Sergio Agüero.

6. The MSN Era and a Second Treble

Barcelona signed Uruguayan striker Luis Suárez in the summer of 2014 and, alongside Messi and Brazilian forward Neymar Jr., formed an attacking trio nicknamed "MSN." Under coach Luis Enrique, the trio combined for well over 100 goals in the 2014-15 season alone, powering Barcelona to a second treble. The Champions League final that year was played at the Olympiastadion in Berlin on June 6, 2015, where Barcelona beat Juventus 3-1, with Messi, Suárez, and Neymar all involved in the scoring.

Neymar left Barcelona for Paris Saint-Germain in a world-record transfer in August 2017, ending the MSN partnership. Messi continued to lead Barcelona through the following seasons under coaches Ernesto Valverde and later Quique Setién, extending his personal collection of individual honors, including a record sixth Ballon d'Or in December 2019.

7. Copa América Triumph and Leaving Barcelona

Under new Argentina coach Lionel Scaloni, and alongside a new generation of teammates including Rodrigo De Paul, Emiliano Martínez, and Ángel Di María, Messi finally won his first major title with the senior national team at the 2021 Copa América, held in Brazil due to pandemic postponement. In the final on July 10, 2021, at the Maracanã Stadium, Argentina beat host nation Brazil 1-0 thanks to a first-half goal from Di María.

Weeks later, in August 2021, Barcelona announced that Messi would leave the club after failing to register his renewed contract under La Liga's financial fair play rules amid the club's severe debt crisis. On August 10, 2021, Paris Saint-Germain announced the signing of Messi on a free transfer, reuniting him with former Barcelona teammate Neymar and pairing him with young French striker Kylian Mbappé, under head coach Mauricio Pochettino.

8. The PSG Chapter

Messi's two seasons in Paris, from 2021 to 2023, included league titles but also criticism for underwhelming form in his first season, in which he was jeered by sections of the Parc des Princes crowd, and disappointment in the Champions League, where PSG was eliminated in the round of 16 in both 2022 and 2023 despite fielding Messi, Neymar, and Mbappé together. Christophe Galtier replaced Pochettino as head coach for the 2022-23 season. Messi's time at PSG was interrupted, and ultimately defined, by his performances for Argentina at the end of 2022.

9. World Cup Glory in Qatar

At the 2022 FIFA World Cup in Qatar, Messi, by then thirty-five years old and playing in what he described as his final World Cup, led Argentina through the tournament alongside teammates including Julián Álvarez, Enzo Fernández, Emiliano Martínez, and captain's-armband successor moments aside, wore the captaincy himself throughout. After an opening defeat to Saudi Arabia, Argentina won every remaining match. In the final on December 18, 2022, at Lusail Stadium, Argentina met France, led by Mbappé, Messi's PSG teammate. Messi scored twice, in the 23rd minute and the 108th minute, while Mbappé scored a hat-trick for France, sending the match to a penalty shootout after a 3-3 draw. Argentina won the shootout 4-2, with Emiliano Martínez saving two French penalties, and Messi lifted the World Cup trophy for the first time in his career. He was again named winner of the Golden Ball as the tournament's outstanding player, becoming the first man to win the award twice.

10. A New Chapter in Miami

Messi's PSG contract expired in June 2023, and speculation mounted over a potential return to Barcelona or a lucrative move to Saudi Arabia's Al-Hilal. Instead, on June 7, 2023, Messi announced he would join Inter Miami CF in Major League Soccer, a club partly owned by former England captain David Beckham. He made his debut on July 21, 2023, in a Leagues Cup group-stage match against Cruz Azul, coming on as a substitute and scoring a dramatic stoppage-time free kick to win the match 2-1, an entrance later described by commentators as one of the most memorable debuts in the sport's history.

Playing under coach Gerardo "Tata" Martino, who had also coached both Barcelona and the Argentina national team earlier in his career, Messi inspired an immediate turnaround at Inter Miami, guiding the club to the Leagues Cup title in August 2023, its first trophy. He was soon reunited with several former teammates, as Barcelona veterans Sergio Busquets and Jordi Alba, and later Luis Suárez, also signed for Inter Miami, recreating pieces of the old Barcelona core in South Florida. In October 2023, Messi won a record eighth Ballon d'Or, extending a lead in the award that no other player in the sport's history has approached. In July 2024, Messi again started the Copa América for Argentina under Scaloni but left the final against Colombia with an ankle injury before Argentina won 1-0 in extra time through a goal from Lautaro Martínez at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens on July 14, 2024.

From a small-statured boy in Rosario receiving injections he could barely afford, to a Barcelona academy graduate mentored by Ronaldinho, to a World Cup champion and Inter Miami's marquee attraction, Messi's career spanned two decades, three continents, and a cast of teammates, coaches, and rivals nearly unmatched in the history of the sport.
